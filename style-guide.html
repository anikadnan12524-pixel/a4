The modern style guide

The principles behind great outfits: colour, proportion, fit, shoes and accessories, explained simply so you can style anything you own.

Chapter one

Colour that works

Most outfits look good when they’re built on neutrals with one or two colours on top. Neutrals include black, white, grey, navy, beige, camel and olive.

  • Tonal: different shades of one colour, such as cream, sand and camel, look calm and expensive.
  • Complementary: colours opposite on the wheel, like navy and rust, create confident contrast.
  • Analogous: neighbours on the wheel, such as olive and mustard, feel harmonious.
  • The 60/30/10 split: one main colour, one supporting colour and a small accent.

Chapter two

Proportion and fit

Proportion is about balancing volume. If one piece is loose, keep the other closer to the body, and think about where the eye is drawn.

  • Loose top, slim bottom or slim top, wide bottom.
  • Define the waist with a tuck, a belt or a cropped layer.
  • Mind the hem: cropped trousers pair well with low shoes; long, wide trousers need a little heel or a chunky sole.
  • Tailoring helps: taking up sleeves or trousers is often the quickest upgrade.

Chapter four

Accessories, edited

  • One focal point: statement earrings or a bold bag, not both.
  • Match your metals loosely, or mix them deliberately in two or three pieces.
  • Belts finish tucked shirts and give shape to coats and dresses.
  • Scarves add colour near the face and work all year.
  • Hats and sunglasses are practical and instantly make an outfit look considered.
Quick checklist

Before you leave the house

  • Does everything fit and sit properly when you move?
  • Is there one clear focal point?
  • Do the colours share a theme?
  • Are your shoes clean and right for the day?
  • Do you feel comfortable and like yourself?
